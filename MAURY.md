# Matthew Fontaine Maury (1806-1873)

**"Pathfinder of the Seas" / "Father of Modern Oceanography"**

## Why Maury for a Cartography Package

Maury pioneered **data-driven cartography**. He collected ship logs from captains worldwide, aggregated thousands of observations, and produced charts that revealed patterns invisible to individual sailors. This is exactly the pipeline pattern we built: acquire data from multiple sources, process and combine it, render useful visualizations.

## Key Accomplishments

- **Wind and Current Charts** (1847-1855) - Revolutionary navigation charts based on aggregated ship log data
- **The Physical Geography of the Sea** (1855) - First comprehensive oceanography textbook, written in accessible English
- **First depth map of the North Atlantic** (1854) - Enabled laying of the transatlantic telegraph cable
- **Uniform system of recording oceanographic data** - Established international standards at 1853 Brussels Conference

## Writing Style

Maury wrote for both scientific and popular audiences. His letters home describe his work in personal, accessible terms. He lectured extensively at universities and scientific societies, developing a clear explanatory voice.

From *The Physical Geography of the Sea* (1855):
> "There is a river in the ocean. In the severest droughts it never fails, and in the mightiest floods it never overflows. Its banks and its bottom are of cold water, while its current is of warm. The Gulf of Mexico is its fountain, and its mouth is in the Arctic Seas. It is the Gulf Stream."

## Philosophical Fit

Maury's approach mirrors our project:

| Maury's Method | Our Pipeline |
|----------------|--------------|
| Collect ship logs from many captains | Acquire data from Census, CanVec, Quebec |
| Aggregate observations by location | Process and merge features |
| Identify patterns (currents, winds) | Create water cutouts, identify islands |
| Produce navigational charts | Render SVG, PDF, web maps |
| Multiple chart types for different uses | Multiple output formats and quality levels |
