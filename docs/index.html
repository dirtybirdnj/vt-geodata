How It Works

The layer sandwich that makes the map

The Basic Idea

This project takes geographic data from government sources (US Census, Canada, Quebec, Vermont) and combines it into layered maps. Think of it like stacking transparent sheets — each layer adds something (boundaries, water, roads) and they combine into a complete picture.

The Water Cutout Trick

The key technique is "water cutouts." Town boundaries normally cover everything, including lakes. We use geometry to subtract lake shapes from town shapes, leaving holes. When you stack the layers, the blue water shows through these holes. This is how Lake Champlain appears correctly with the Champlain Islands visible as land.

The Layer Sandwich (Top to Bottom)

Click any layer name to preview it. Layers render bottom-to-top, so highways appear on top of everything.

24-27 Highways (on top)
interstates us_highways quebec_highways state_routes
16-23 Small Water (shows through town cutouts)
vt_lakes vt_rivers quebec_lakes ny_lakes nh_lakes ma_lakes
10-15 Town Boundaries (with water cutouts)
vt_towns quebec ny_towns nh_towns ma_towns me_towns
6-9 Major Water Bodies
lake_champlain memphremagog richelieu missisquoi
1-5 Regional Backgrounds (bottom)
quebec ny nh ma me

Why this order? Major water bodies sit below town boundaries so islands appear as holes. Small rivers/lakes sit above towns so they show through the cutout holes. Roads go on top of everything.

Data Sources

  • US Census TIGER — Boundaries, roads, water for US states
  • CanVec — Canadian water features
  • Quebec Open Data — Municipal boundaries and highways
  • Vermont Open Geodata — State boundary and detailed water

Output Formats

  • SVG — Vector files for pen plotters (ultra_fine, fine, medium)
  • Web — Interactive Leaflet maps
  • PDF — Print-ready output (planned)
