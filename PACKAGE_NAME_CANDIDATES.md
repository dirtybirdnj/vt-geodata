# Package Name Candidates

Evaluating historical cartographers/mapmakers for the package name. Key criteria:
- Accessible writings in English (for narrative voice)
- Philosophical fit with our data pipeline approach
- Clean, memorable package name
- Available on PyPI

---

## 1. Matthew Fontaine Maury (1806-1873)

**Package name:** `maury` - AVAILABLE on PyPI

**Title:** "Pathfinder of the Seas" / "Father of Oceanography"

**Why him:**
- Pioneered **data-driven cartography** - collected ship logs from captains worldwide, aggregated data, produced charts
- Wrote *The Physical Geography of the Sea* (1855) - first oceanography textbook, accessible English prose
- Created wind/current charts that revolutionized navigation

**Accessible sources:**
- [Library of Congress: 14,650 digitized items](https://www.loc.gov/collections/matthew-fontaine-maury-papers/) including personal letters, diaries, speeches
- Lectured at universities - clear explanatory voice

**Sample quote:**
> "There is a river in the ocean. In the severest droughts it never fails, and in the mightiest floods it never overflows."

**Fit with our project:** ★★★★★
His method (collect data from many sources → aggregate → find patterns → produce charts) mirrors our pipeline exactly.

## 2. Alexander von Humboldt (1769-1859)

**Package name:** `humboldt` - AVAILABLE on PyPI

**Title:** "Father of Ecology" / Scientific explorer

**Why him:**
- Invented **isothermal lines** (1817) - first thematic map innovation
- Wrote 36 books, 25,000 letters to correspondents worldwide
- Created revolutionary maps of Mexico/New Spain with explicit methodology notes
- Used maps as tools of scientific exploration, not just literal representation

**Accessible sources:**
- [American Philosophical Society: English writings collection](https://www.amphilsoc.org/publications/alexander-von-humboldt-writings-english-part-i-1789-1824) - 250 texts in English
- Extensive correspondence with American cartographers

**Challenge:** Much of his major work (*Cosmos*, *Critical Examination*) still lacks English translation

**Sample approach:**
> "Humboldt was unusually explicit about how he carried out his task. The 'Geographical Introduction' to his Political Essay on the Kingdom of New Spain contains extensive information about how he compiled this map."

**Fit with our project:** ★★★★☆
Pioneered thematic mapping and data visualization, but less data-pipeline focused than Maury.

## 3. Henry David Thoreau (1817-1862)

**Package name:** `thoreau` - AVAILABLE on PyPI

**Title:** Surveyor, naturalist, author of *Walden*

**Why him:**
- Professional land surveyor - supported himself surveying while writing
- Created **200 land surveys** including three of Walden Pond
- Surveyed 26 square miles of Concord in exceptional detail
- Kept 2-million-word journal over 24 years combining observation with measurement

**Accessible sources:**
- [Concord Free Public Library: 200 surveys + field notes](https://concordlibrary.org/special-collections/fin_aids/HDT)
- [Walden Woods Project: Complete writings digitized](https://www.walden.org/what-we-do/library/thoreau/the-writings-of-henry-david-thoreau-the-digital-collection/)
- *Walden* included his survey map of the pond

**Sample quote (from Walden):**
> "I surveyed it carefully... I fathomed it easily with a cod-line and a stone weighing about a pound and a half, and could tell accurately when the stone left the bottom."

**Fit with our project:** ★★★☆☆
Beautiful writer, careful surveyor, but his work was local/observational rather than systematic data aggregation.

## 4. William Smith (1769-1839)

**Package name:** `strata` - AVAILABLE on PyPI

**Title:** "Father of English Geology"

**Why him:**
- Created **first nationwide geological map** (1815) - 8 feet tall, 6 feet wide
- Invented the concept that **layers (strata) could be identified by their contents** (fossils)
- Self-taught, worked as canal surveyor, developed methods through fieldwork
- Used graded coloring technique - darker at base of each stratum

**Accessible sources:**
- [Oxford Natural History Museum: Complete archive](https://oumnh.ox.ac.uk/william-smith) - diaries, letters, charts, plans
- Published *Strata Identified by Organized Fossils* (1816-1819)
- *Memoir to the Map* (1815)

**Challenge:** Working-class background, less polished prose than academics

**Fit with our project:** ★★★★☆
Layer-based thinking is perfect metaphor! "Strata" as package name directly describes what we do.

## 5. Florence Kelley (1859-1932)

**Package name:** `kelley` - AVAILABLE on PyPI

**Title:** Social reformer, pioneer of social mapping

**Why her:**
- Directed the **Hull House Maps and Papers** (1895) - first systematic investigation of an American neighborhood
- Created maps showing nationality and wages block-by-block in Chicago
- Used maps to reveal patterns invisible in raw data - poverty as systemic, not individual failure
- Methodology influenced Chicago School sociology

**Accessible sources:**
- [Hull House Maps and Papers on Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/hullhousemapspap00unse) - complete book free online
- [Northwestern Florence Kelley Collection](https://florencekelley.northwestern.edu/historical/hullhouse/)
- Essays on "The Sweating-System" and "Wage-earning Children"

**Sample approach:**
> "To collect this information... Florence Kelley and their assistants knocked on doors, went into homes and sweatshops, and asked the residents... questions about their ethnic origins, the number of people in the household, the wages earned..."

**Fit with our project:** ★★★★☆
Data collection → mapping → reveal hidden patterns. First American GIS-style analysis. Also brings gender diversity to the options.

## My Recommendations

### Top Pick: `maury`
Best combination of accessible writings, data-pipeline philosophy, and memorable name.

### Strong Alternative: `strata`
The name itself describes what we do (layer composition). William Smith's underdog story is compelling.

### Wildcard: `kelley`
Brings a different perspective - social mapping, revealing patterns in human data. Modern resonance.
